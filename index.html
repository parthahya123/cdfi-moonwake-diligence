What Moonwake Proposes

An AI-native affiliate lending platform: Moonwake handles marketing, applications, underwriting, closing, servicing, and customer support. Every loan is made on partner CDFI paper, under its credit policy, with its capital. The ask: $75–100M over three years from OpenAI Foundation for technology, labor, and operations.

What Actually Constrains CDFI Lending?

Bottom line: Operational and capital constraints are roughly co-equal barriers. The evidence does NOT support a clean narrative that CDFIs have capital sitting idle waiting for an operational platform. Both problems are real and intertwined.

The 22–30% undeployed capital is not purely "available" — CDFIs maintain prudent liquidity reserves. But high Unrestricted Net Asset ratios (30–37% for business lenders) mean larger CDFIs have balance sheet room to leverage further if they had operational capacity.

Demand Is Not the Problem

  • 71% of CDFIs reported increased demand in 2024; 77% anticipate demand will rise through 2025
  • Only 33% said they could fully meet overall demand
  • Target population: 134 million low-income individuals (42% of the U.S.)
  • Annual small business funding gap: $87B+ (Federal Reserve estimate)
  • The Leviticus Fund: "a $100 million pipeline right now — we don't have $100 million of capital"

The Federal Environment Is Deteriorating

Trump FY2026 budget proposed a 90% cut to the CDFI Fund (from $324M to $33M). OMB has disbursed zero award dollars in FY2026 so far. The proposed $100M "Rural Financial Award Program" would require 60% rural activity — sidelining urban CDFIs. The American Banker: "The CDFI boom is over."

Size Matters: Two Very Different CDFI Realities

Large, well-capitalized CDFIs

  • High UNA ratios → balance sheet headroom
  • Operational constraint is more binding
  • Moonwake's thesis is strongest here
  • Most likely to actually adopt a platform

Small, under-capitalized CDFIs (majority)

  • Capital is the binding constraint
  • Platform won't help if they can't lend
  • Too resource-constrained to implement new tech
  • 72% struggle with tech integration even with free grants

AI Underwriting: What the Evidence Actually Shows

❌

The "10x Approval Rate" Claim

No study, regulatory filing, or peer-reviewed paper documents anything close to a 10x (1,000%) approval rate increase from AI underwriting. The best documented real-world gain is 27–44% (Upstart/CFPB no-action letter, 2017–2022). A CDFI approval rate of 27% × 10 = 270% — mathematically impossible.

What "10x" likely actually means: Judi.ai's Vancity case study shows processing time fell from ~4 hours to ~5 minutes — a ~48x throughput improvement. Loans funded increased 330% (3.3x). These are real gains, but they're throughput metrics, not approval rate metrics. Conflating them is either sloppy or misleading.

Fair Lending Risk

CFPB Circular 2023-03: AI "black box" decisions require specific, accurate adverse action notices. Generic checklist reasons are not acceptable. Generating compliant ECOA adverse action notices from ML models is "the most technically difficult explainability challenge" for lenders (FinRegLab).
Massachusetts AG v. Earnest (July 2025): $2.5M settlement for AI underwriting producing disparate impact on Black, Hispanic, and non-citizen applicants. The model used a "Cohort Default Rate" variable that functioned as a racial proxy. The company never tested its models for disparate impact. CDFIs' borrower bases are predominantly minority — this risk is acute.

Who Bears the Credit Risk?

This is the central structural problem. Moonwake controls underwriting decisions but takes zero credit risk. CDFIs bear all losses on their balance sheets. If the AI model fails under stress (as Upstart's did in 2022), CDFIs absorb the consequences. With a median self-sufficiency ratio of 35% and business lender charge-offs already at a "review period high" of 4.7%, CDFIs cannot absorb a large loss spike.

CDFI Technology Landscape

The gap is real — no comprehensive AI-native lending platform exists for CDFIs today. But the market isn't empty, and the industry is moving. A $75–100M greenfield build faces adoption risk and competes with emerging purpose-built tools.

OFN Innovation Initiative — The Industry's Own Effort

OFN + MassMutual Foundation announced a $25M partnership in October 2024, running 2025–2029. The initiative covers five pillars: capital, product expansion, policy, talent, and technology. However, the first deliverable — the Capital Solutions Accelerator Demo Day (April 2026) — focused entirely on capitalization solutions, not technology platforms. The technology pillar is the least developed so far. Mastercard Strive is a co-partner.

Diligence Questions to Demand Answers To

  • What specific, measurable lending technology has Moonwake actually built? Where is it deployed today?
  • What exactly does "10x approval rate" mean? Provide the methodology and source.
  • Who bears credit losses when AI underwriting produces elevated defaults? What contractual protections do CDFIs have?
  • Has outside counsel reviewed the true lender doctrine risk in the proposed affiliate structure?
  • Has any CDFI signed a binding letter of intent to participate?
  • How does this compare to funding Lendistry, SPARK/Arcadea, or OFN's Innovation Initiative at the same cost?
  • What is Moonwake's plan if CFPB or a state AG determines the CDFI is not the true lender?
⚠️

Recommendation

Do not fund as proposed. The problem is real and important. But the "10x" claim is false and disqualifying; the entity appears misrepresented; and the credit risk architecture (tech vendor takes none, CDFIs take all) creates a moral hazard a philanthropy should not underwrite. If the OpenAI Foundation wants to move capital into CDFI technology, better-targeted alternatives exist that could reach more institutions with less risk.
